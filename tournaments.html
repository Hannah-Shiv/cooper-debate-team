New to the circuit?

WASDL & Middle School Public Forum

Cooper competes in the Washington Area Speech and Debate League (WASDL), a regional, all-volunteer circuit where middle school students from Maryland, Virginia, and Washington, D.C. debate current issues throughout the season.

Our local circuit

WASDL brings area schools together for regular-season tournaments and Metro Finals. It gives Cooper debaters a consistent place to research, practice, compete, and grow with students from across the region.

The format we compete in

Middle School Public Forum is a two-on-two format focused on current events and public policy. Teams prepare to argue both PRO and CON, using clear explanations, credible evidence, and persuasive reasoning.

What students build

Debate develops research, critical thinking, confident communication, teamwork, and the ability to explain a complex issue to a general audience.

A Public Forum round at a glance

  1. 4 minConstructive
  2. 3 minCrossfire
  3. 4 minRebuttal
  4. 2 minSummary
  5. 2 minFinal Focus

A full round typically takes about 35 minutes, plus preparation time.

Arrival & check-in

For Families

Parent & Family Guide

Everything you need to know about supporting your debater at tournaments

Tournament Day Schedule

WASDL tournaments typically run from 8:00 AM to 5:30 PM. Students should arrive 30 minutes early. Rounds run throughout the day with lunch breaks.

Dress Code

Business professional attire is required for all tournament participants. Boys: suit or dress pants/shirt/tie. Girls: professional suit, dress, or pantsuit.

Transportation

Parents are responsible for transporting students to and from tournaments. Families may set up carpools, but arranging transportation is each family’s responsibility.

Registration & Fees

Families pay $20 per Cooper debater each year. This covers annual WASDL dues and five preliminary WASDL tournaments. Metro Finals or Nationals may add $15–$20 per student.

Communication

Coaches use Talking Points and Schoology for team communication and tournament updates—not the team app.

Watching Rounds

Check with the judge and the other debaters before watching a round. Once invited, remain silent and do not signal to debaters.
